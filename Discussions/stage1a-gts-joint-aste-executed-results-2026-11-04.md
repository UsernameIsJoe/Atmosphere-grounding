## Critical caveat: GTS negation model output was unfairly treated as missing

The actual GTS raw decoder output on the controlled example “The park is not quiet; sirens make the atmosphere stressful.” was **`park → not quiet ;` (positive polarity)**. The extractor's current unique-source-string matcher rejected it since source text says `not quiet;` (no space before semicolon). Thus the numeric benchmark falsely gives GTS no credit for **recognizing the explicit negated phrase**. The positive polarity accompanying this negated observation is still problematic: positive/negative ASTE sentiment tags are not equivalent to negation or atmosphere-direction labels.

**Next engineering correction:** Normalize tokenizer-inserted punctuation spacing for candidate alignment, but preserve exact original source offsets and record normalization; never silently rewrite actual evidence. Re-run the fixed benchmark before final model selection.

## Interpretation

GTS demonstrates **some model capability and a useful improvement in selectivity** over the existing dependency baseline. But its **extremely low coverage** on these diagnostic observations means it cannot replace existing extraction as the sole component. Neither AOS nor GTS is validated as the best place-review ASTE model. The model and matcher must be judged separately.

**Next bounded step:** Fix GTS source-alignment punctuation handling, then run the same frozen controls plus a held-out subset of real reviews with independently reviewed aspect–opinion relationships. Keep relevance and emotion NLI unchanged. Do not declare a winner or aggregate with NLI based on these eight controls alone.
