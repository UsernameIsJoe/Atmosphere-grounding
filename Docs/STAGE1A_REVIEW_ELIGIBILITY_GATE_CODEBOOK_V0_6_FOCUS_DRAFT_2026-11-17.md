# Stage 1A — review-focus-first gate codebook v0.6 (draft, 2026-11-17)

**Status:** Untested draft, not adopted. Derived directly from archived v0.4. The failed v0.5 contextual rewrite and ratio experiment remain archived and are not the base for this revision. Current v0.4 configuration, gate model, and historical decisions are unchanged.

**Strategy change:** v0.4 could KEEP a review because one qualifying observation appeared anywhere. This draft first assesses the main subject of the review's evaluation. A possible anchor is considered only after that review-focus judgment. This is a deliberate eligibility-policy change, not just a rewording of the previous sufficiency test.

## New first step: review focus

Classify the whole review before choosing a quote:

- **place:** its main assessment characterizes being in, entering, using, navigating or experiencing the reviewed location itself.
- **encounter:** its main assessment concerns an event, person, meal, transaction, institutional dispute or another location. Passing venue praise or feature mentions do not establish place focus.
- **mixed:** a place assessment and an encounter assessment are both substantial subjects. Mentioning both is insufficient; the place needs a genuine assessment thread.

The main assessment is what the account evaluates, not where it occurred or which business is named. Do not infer it from an isolated adjective, the word atmosphere, or the existence of an anchorable physical noun. A short review may have clear place focus; a long encounter narrative may contain an incidental place sentence. No length ratio, clause count, majority rule, mandatory repeat visit or blanket event exclusion is used.

**KEEP requires two checks:** place or mixed focus, AND a substantive, source-grounded place observation. Place/mixed focus alone does not rescue generic praise. Encounter focus remains REJECT even if a passing sentence could independently sound place-related. The later anchor must illustrate the established place-assessment thread; it cannot create that thread after the fact.

**Mixed-review policy change and tradeoff:** this draft can reject an encounter-centered review containing a small but useful place observation. That loses some minority evidence that v0.4 would retain. The policy is intended to match the researcher's concern about reviews being rescued by incidental text, but needs explicit review alongside false-kept cases. Do not call the new policy more accurate merely because it rejects a wedding case or more reviews overall. Meaningful mixed reviews with a main place-assessment thread must remain eligible.

## Evidence sufficiency after the focus decision

The gate reads the **entire review at once**; no segmentation or individual clause-by-clause eligibility classification. A source-exact `anchor_quote` is required **only after** a KEEP verdict, to document independently sufficient evidence, **not** to search for a phrase that forces a KEEP verdict.

In deciding, consider the **review's subject and the role of the candidate observation** in its context. Ask whether the purported observation would actually teach us something **meaningful, intelligible, and relevant** about visiting, using, accessing, navigating, or experiencing the reviewed location. A feature merely named in a discussion about an unrelated institutional policy, private transaction, or event does not qualify solely because it is a physical object. General praise without an interpretable underlying quality also fails.

The candidate observation must satisfy **all** of the following:

1. **About the reviewed place:** not another location, individual staff member, unrelated institution, or just an event taking place there.
2. **Substantive and interpretable:** an actual specific place attribute, condition, atmosphere, usability, spatial experience or visitor-facing practice, rather than an unqualified superlative (e.g. "awesome", "great") or unsupported generic convenience.
3. **Contextually relevant:** a meaningful description of that place as a place. Do not count tangential mentions of a behind-the-scenes room or feature when used solely in an argument about food provision, staff conduct or organizational policies.

**Mixed reviews:** first establish that place assessment is a main thread alongside the encounter. A passing observation cannot independently rescue encounter focus. A short mixed review can still qualify when both assessments are substantive. Do not count words or require repeat visits. Apply this focus rule before the evidence-sufficiency examples below.

**Disambiguation cases:**

| Review content | Gate | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Shelter food-quality and rejected-donation complaint; incidental "they have a restaurant-grade kitchen" | REJECT | Kitchen is mentioned to criticize food administration, not as meaningful visitor-facing place experience |
| Apartment lease and praise of a particular agent; "the property is awesome and the area is convenient" | REJECT | Neither phrase conveys a substantive, interpretable site quality; generic property praise is insufficient |
| "The restroom is cramped and its sink does not work" | KEEP | Specific, experienced spatial/facility condition of the place under review |
| "The park is peaceful and shaded" | KEEP | Meaningful atmospheric and environmental characteristics even in a short review |
| "The restaurant forgot our food, but the patio is quiet and spacious" | KEEP | Independent place-directed evidence despite a service complaint |

**Scope boundary:** the new focus criterion applies generally to event, meal, service and transaction narratives; it is not an exception for a named review or occasion type.

## Core test: place-level versus individual encounter

**Place-level evidence** reports a spatial, environmental, sensory, functional, atmospheric, access, or visitor-experience **characteristic of the location being reviewed**. It need not be objectively permanent or universal; a specific visitor's experience can qualify if it meaningfully reveals the character, conditions, use, accessibility, atmosphere or establishment-level service of the place.

**Isolated personal encounter** concerns a particular person, transaction, gratitude, unique private mishap or private occasion without a defensible place-level characterization. Its occurrence *at* a place does not make it evidence *about* the place.

The distinction is about **what the review tells us**, not whether it uses "I", concerns people, or describes a single visit.

## Binary outputs

- **KEEP** when place or mixed focus is established and a **meaningful, text-grounded passage** characterizes the **reviewed location** as a place to enter, use, experience, visit, stay, navigate or avoid. Place qualities include design/layout, ambience, sound/light/crowds, access and facilities, visitor-facing conditions, attractiveness, and discernible establishment-level hospitality/service practices.
- **REJECT** when encounter focus is established, or when the review has **no** substantive place-level evidence. Examples: thanks to a named waitress for personal help; one particular server's friendliness absent any broader characterization; a private appointment delay; history-only narration; descriptions/ratings of individual art pieces/exhibits without appraising the visit or environment; impressions of *another* location rather than the one reviewed.
- **MIXED:** KEEP only when the place is genuinely a main assessment subject alongside the encounter and qualifying evidence is present. Do not assign mixed merely because both topics appear. Segmentation cannot retrospectively justify passing a review whose focus is encounter. But incidental mentions such as "we took photos there", "I was here", or "the staff did not complain" do **not** automatically elevate a purely personal story to place-level evidence.
- **AMBIGUOUS:** Prefer KEEP only when both place/mixed focus and substantive evidence are identifiable; otherwise REJECT. Do not manufacture a place-level generalization to avoid a false negative.
- **Scope:** The "place" is the **venue/site actually being reviewed**. The venue may be a park, station, square, store, gallery, museum, restaurant or restroom. Avoid substituting a reviewer's comments about another venue.

## Narrow clarification: individual food and service outcomes (v0.3)

The quality of **one dish or meal**, the outcome of **one payment or private transaction**, and **one isolated service success/failure** (including a waiter/manager's responsiveness) are **not independently place-level evidence**. Being served food or encountering staff at an establishment does not by itself characterize its atmosphere, spatial qualities, accessibility, or general hospitality.

**KEEP** a mixed review when place assessment is a main thread and it explicitly describes a substantive quality of the reviewed place, such as an inviting atmosphere, a comfortable patio, the organization of customer-facing space, accessibility, or an established/recurring service practice. **Do not** promote a one-time meal or complaint into a general statement about the venue's service culture. Likewise do **not** reject a true environmental observation just because a waiter, meal, or one visit also appears.

| Example | Gate | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Our dinner arrived cold, and the waitress never checked on us." | REJECT | Isolated food and service failure only |
| "The cake was delicious and the portions were huge." | REJECT | Food evaluation without place-level observation |
| "Our meal was disappointing, but the patio was spacious and peaceful." | KEEP | Explicit place-level patio condition independent of the meal |
| "The restaurant feels warm and welcoming, and its servers are consistently attentive." | KEEP | Establishment-level atmosphere and service practice |

Evaluate event-bound atmosphere statements using review focus before applying sufficiency. Neither event language nor a positive atmosphere adjective determines the verdict alone.

## Visitor-facing people: precise boundary

| Evidence | Gate | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "This store feels welcoming; staff generally make visitors feel at ease" | KEEP | General establishment-level experience |
| "The servers here are routinely attentive even when the place is busy" | KEEP | Repeated/establishment-level service practice |
| "Thank you to waitress Anna for helping with my birthday surprise" | REJECT | Praise of one named person/private event only |
| "One cashier was very kind when my card failed" | REJECT | Isolated transaction |
| "The posted entry policy prevented visitors from using the entrance" | KEEP | Repeatable access rule of place |
| "People here are regularly confronted by a neighboring resident" | KEEP | Reported recurring place-facing condition; do not claim universality beyond text |

One observed incident **can** qualify if it discloses a physical hazard, posted rule, systemic access condition or directly experienced place characteristic; count neither singularity nor interpersonal language as an automatic reject or keep.

## Artwork and history

- **REJECT:** An explanation of a monument's history; reviews solely rating art pieces or exhibitions, without comments on the experience of the reviewed place.
- **KEEP:** "The gallery lighting made it difficult to see the works"; "the museum is enjoyable to explore with children"; a site/history discussion also containing substantive reports about how the place feels or works.
- Praise for a **different** location is not evidence about the reviewed site.

## Historical calibration

The archived v0.4 case-level calibration remains development history, not new examples or independent gold. Do not send case IDs, expected judgments or prior outputs to the model. Re-audit both known false keeps and useful mixed reviews under the explicit focus-policy change; do not force historical labels by inserting literal cases into the new prompt. Existing general v0.4 illustrations are retained as inherited development guidance, so same-batch results still cannot establish held-out performance.

## Evaluation controls

Use only the full source review; no selected segment or prior emotion label. Output review_focus and a one-sentence focus_summary FIRST, then binary keep, source-exact anchor for KEEP (empty for REJECT), and a short reason connecting focus to sufficiency. No emotion verdicts, E/T, ratios or segment labels. Check exact quote substring mechanically. A short anchor demonstrates eligibility but is not a substitute for reading full context.

**Success:** Very high recall on genuinely eligible reviews **without** automatically keeping every review that mentions a venue, employee, history or exhibit. Specifically evaluate false-kept personal encounters and false-rejected first-person place experiences. Run a balanced, independently adjudicated set with authentic negative reviews before reporting meaningful precision/recall.

## Draft output schema and implementation boundary

Use configs/stage1a_review_eligibility_gate_v0_6_focus_draft.json. The two additional fields are review_focus (place/encounter/mixed) and focus_summary. KEEP with encounter focus is inconsistent and must fail mechanical validation. KEEP also requires a nonempty exact anchor; REJECT an empty anchor. The focus judgment itself needs semantic audit; a valid enum does not prove it is right.

The existing three-field v0.4 runner/validator cannot be reused unchanged. No new runner, model run, adoption or current-config change is part of this draft. A future controlled test should compare directly to frozen v0.4 with unchanged gate model/settings and all the same review inputs, inspect focus errors and new false rejections, and include fresh real reviews before making generalization claims. Gemma remains the primary downstream emotion model; Qwen remains available for comparison. That separate policy is unchanged.
