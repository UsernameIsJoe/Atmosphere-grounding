PHASE 1 — Top-level visual categories (v1)
Atmosphere Grounding project — image descriptive ontology
Date: 2026-08-11
Status: LOCKED for v1 (schema designed to allow later category additions)
================================================================================

PURPOSE
-------
Define a fixed set of top-level categories that describe HOW we read an image.
Categories are a descriptive ontology for visual features. They are NOT bound
to atmosphere lexicon labels (inviting / safe / calm / …). Atmosphere links
are left for the grounding pipeline to discover from data.

Unit of description: primarily IMAGE; PLACE = aggregation of its images.
Flexibility: categories fixed for v1; schema marks extensible=true for v2+.


CURATION METHOD (this phase)
----------------------------
Pass 1 of three:
  1) Top-level categories (this file)
  2) Subtype families under each category
  3) Extractor tags per subtype family

Selection criteria for a top-level category:
  - Supported by urban / ambiance / environmental-psychology / visual theory
  - Visually decidable from place photos (parks, plazas, open spaces)
  - Useful as a dimension in a multi-feature space for later verification
  - No predefined mapping to atmosphere lexicon terms


DECISION SUMMARY (v1 top-level list)
------------------------------------
KEEP (12 categories):

  id                        name
  ------------------------- ----------------------------
  objects_furnishings       Objects & furnishings
  vegetation                Vegetation
  circulation_access        Circulation & access
  boundaries_edges          Boundaries & edges
  facade_built_form         Facade & built form
  light                     Light
  materiality_surface       Materiality & surface
  water_terrain             Water & terrain
  social_presence           Social presence
  spatial_structure         Spatial structure
  composition_contrast      Composition & contrast
  wayfinding_information    Wayfinding & information

REJECTED / DEFERRED merges for v1 (kept separate for theory clarity):
  - Did NOT fold water_terrain into vegetation/materiality (distinct restorative
    and landscape-design literature; common in park/plaza photos).
  - Did NOT fold wayfinding into circulation (Lynch separates landmarks/signs
    from paths; useful for legibility without assuming atmosphere).
  - Sound / smell / climate comfort: theoretically important to ambiance
    (Böhme, Thibaud, Gehl) but not reliably available from still photos →
    OUT OF SCOPE for v1 image ontology (document for multimodal v2).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CATEGORY RATIONALES + LITERATURE SUPPORT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1) objects_furnishings — Objects & furnishings
   Decision: KEEP as core discrete equipment layer (seating, play, amenities).
   Why: Public-life and comfort literature treat furnishings as primary stay
   affordances, independent of overall enclosure or material.
   Support:
   - Gehl, J. (2010). Cities for People. Island Press. Twelve Quality Criteria
     under Comfort: opportunities to sit, stand, see, talk, play/exercise.
     Tool overview: https://civitas.eu/tool-inventory/twelve-quality-criteria
     Gehl Architects worksheet: https://gehlpeople.com/tools/twelve-quality-criteria/
   - Whyte, W. H. (1980). The Social Life of Small Urban Spaces. Conservation
     Foundation. (seating, ledges, stay features in plazas)
   CV note: Strong fit for open-vocab object detection (Grounding DINO).


2) vegetation — Vegetation
   Decision: KEEP as living plant structure (trees, lawn, plantings).
   Why: Greenery is a major human-centric streetscape/park dimension and is
   measured separately from built mass in CV streetscape work.
   Support:
   - Kaplan, R., & Kaplan, S. (1989). The Experience of Nature. Cambridge UP.
     (restorative / preference role of natural content)
   - Wang, R., et al. (2019). Urban greenery and mental wellbeing… arXiv:1905.04488
     https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04488
   - Middel, A., et al. (2019). Urban form and composition of street canyons…
     Landscape and Urban Planning. DOI: 10.1016/j.landurbplan.2018.12.001
     (trees as a primary spherical fraction class)
   CV note: Instance phrases via DINO; cover via semantic segmentation.


3) circulation_access — Circulation & access
   Decision: KEEP for how movement and thresholds are structured.
   Why: Paths and access structure orientation and exploration; Lynch’s
   “paths” are primary imageability elements.
   Support:
   - Lynch, K. (1960). The Image of the City. MIT Press. (paths as dominant
     element of city image)
   - Gehl (2010), Comfort: opportunities to walk / accessibility.
   CV note: path, walkway, stairs, entrance — DINO; hardscape area — seg.


4) boundaries_edges — Boundaries & edges
   Decision: KEEP for what closes, frames, or excludes space.
   Why: Edges are a Lynch element; barriers are central to safety/exclusion
   discussions (CPTED, hostile design) without forcing an atmosphere label.
   Support:
   - Lynch (1960). Edges as linear boundaries / breaks in continuity.
   - Appleton, J. (1975). The Experience of Landscape. Wiley.
     (refuge / enclosure-related preference; barriers as spatial cover)
   - CPTED literature on territorial definition / access control (e.g. Newman,
     O. (1972). Defensible Space; later CPTED guides on fencing, gates).
   CV note: fence, wall, gate, railing, hedge — strong DINO targets.


5) facade_built_form — Facade & built form
   Decision: KEEP buildings/frontage as visual-spatial mass and edge condition.
   Why: Building walls define enclosure and “soft/hard” edges of public space;
   distinct from freestanding park objects.
   Support:
   - Gehl (2010). Scale; edges of space; building frontage at human scale.
   - Middel et al. (2019). Buildings as a primary composition class in
     pedestrian-centric street imagery.
   CV note: building/facade/door/window phrases (DINO) + building pixel share (seg).


6) light — Light
   Decision: KEEP as illumination cues (fixtures + brightness properties).
   Why: Böhme treats light as a primary atmosphere *generator*; CPTED/Gehl
   treat lighting as protection/comfort — still without binding to “safe”.
   Support:
   - Böhme, G. (1993). Atmosphere as the Fundamental Concept of a New
     Aesthetics. Thesis Eleven, 36, 113–126.
   - Böhme, G.; Thibaud, J.-P. (ed.) (2017). The Aesthetics of Atmospheres.
     Routledge. (sections on seeing light / phenomenology of light)
   - Böhme on generators (light & sound as key producers of atmospheres):
     “The theory of atmospheres and its applications,” IJARA translation.
     https://doi.org/10.24135/ijara.v0i0.480
   - Gehl (2010). Protection against crime/violence — lighting as environmental
     factor in public-life quality criteria practice.
   CV note: fixtures via DINO; brightness via image metrics; “well-lit” soft
   phrases via SigLIP. Do not rely on DINO alone for “quality of light.”


7) materiality_surface — Materiality & surface
   Decision: KEEP materials/surfaces as their own dimension.
   Why: Atmosphere theory emphasizes material ecstasies (how materials radiate
   into space), not only object identity (a wooden vs metal bench differs).
   Support:
   - Böhme (1993; 2017). Things/materials as generators; ecstasies of objects
     into space (production aesthetics of atmospheres).
   - Zumthor, P. (2006). Atmospheres. Birkhäuser. (materials, surfaces, and
     presence in architectural atmosphere — design literature companion)
   CV note: phrase-level DINO (“brick wall”, “wooden bench”, “glass facade”)
   plus segmentation for paving vs soft ground where available.


8) water_terrain — Water & terrain
   Decision: KEEP as landscape anchors beyond vegetation cover.
   Why: Water features and landform are common park/plaza cues and often
   treated separately from trees/lawn in design and restoration literature.
   Support:
   - Kaplan & Kaplan (1989). Water as preferred / restorative landscape content
     in environmental preference research (general finding across preference
     studies).
   - Gehl (2010). Enjoyment / sensory quality; water often used as stay/look
     attractor in public-space design practice.
   CV note: fountain, pond, etc. via DINO; water pixels via seg when present.


9) social_presence — Social presence
   Decision: KEEP people/activity traces as a visual dimension.
   Why: Classic urban social science: presence of others shapes perceived
   public life; still descriptive (counts/presence), not an atmosphere label.
   Support:
   - Jacobs, J. (1961). The Death and Life of Great American Cities.
     (“eyes on the street”)
   - Whyte (1980). People as the primary measure of successful plazas.
   - Gehl (2010). Public life; optional activities; play/exercise.
   - Middel et al. (2019). “Non-permanent objects” class includes people/
     vehicles as transient scene content.
   CV note: people, bicycle via DINO; optional counting.


10) spatial_structure — Spatial structure (enclosure / prospect amounts)
    Decision: KEEP as scene-level spatial amounts (sky/building/veg/ground).
    Why: Prospect–refuge and enclosure/openness are geometric/perceptual
    properties measured as extents, not single object boxes.
    Support:
    - Appleton, J. (1975). The Experience of Landscape. (prospect & refuge)
    - Dosen, A. S., & Ostwald, M. J. (2016). Evidence for prospect-refuge
      theory: a meta-analysis… City, Territory and Architecture.
      https://doi.org/10.1186/s40410-016-0033-1
    - Middel et al. (2019). Spherical fractions: sky, trees, buildings,
      impervious, pervious.
    - Gong et al. / Li et al. street-view SVF–TVF–BVF line of work, e.g.
      mapping sky/tree/building view factors from GSV + deep learning.
    CV note: PRIMARY = semantic segmentation ratios; SigLIP soft phrases
    optional. NOT a DINO-only category.


11) composition_contrast — Composition & contrast
    Decision: KEEP Gestalt / information properties of the frame.
    Why: Kaplan preference matrix separates coherence/complexity (immediate
    2D informational properties) from deeper spatial inference.
    Support:
    - Kaplan, S. (1979). Perception and landscape: conceptions and
      misconceptions. In Proceedings of Our National Landscape (USDA).
      https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/27585
      (coherence, complexity, legibility, mystery)
    - Kaplan, R., & Kaplan, S. (1989). The Experience of Nature.
    - Recent operationalization of Kaplan visual attributes in urban parks:
      e.g. discrete-choice studies on coherence/complexity attributes
      (Sustainability 2022, 14(12), 7357). https://doi.org/10.3390/su14127357
    CV note: PRIMARY = image metrics (contrast, color variance, edge density);
    SigLIP soft phrases optional. NOT a DINO-only category.


12) wayfinding_information — Wayfinding & information
    Decision: KEEP signs/landmarks as orientation aids.
    Why: Lynch landmarks and Kaplan legibility concern finding one’s way;
    visually localizable without presupposing “legible” atmosphere.
    Support:
    - Lynch (1960). Landmarks as point references in the city image.
    - Kaplan preference matrix: legibility as inferred 3D wayfinding ease
      (Kaplan 1979; Kaplan & Kaplan 1989).
    CV note: sign, map board, distinctive landmark objects — DINO.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXPLICIT NON-GOALS OF PHASE 1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- No atmosphere lexicon bindings (inviting/safe/calm/…).
- No full Grounding DINO word lists (Phase 2 = families; later = phrases).
- No extractor implementation details beyond high-level CV feasibility notes
  (Phase 3 assigns extractors formally).
- No audio/olfactory/thermal categories in v1 (photo constraint).


LOCKED TOOL STACK (from prior project decision; referenced here)
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Grounding DINO — localizable noun phrases / boxes
- Semantic segmentation — sky/building/vegetation/ground(/water) fractions
- SigLIP — soft scene phrases + atmosphere weights at test time
- Image metrics — contrast, brightness, complexity proxies
